PROJECT OVERVIEW
---------------
A general-purpose AI platform for simulating societies of autonomous agents
that learn and adapt through behavioral evolution.


WHERE THE PROJECT STANDS
------------------------
The engine is built, not sketched. All four originally planned phases have
been implemented, and a default simulation now runs a society that feeds
itself, waters itself, shelters from the weather, and reproduces over tens of
thousands of ticks without intervention.

What remains is not "write the systems" but "connect what exists": several
analytics components are libraries nobody calls, and agents still cannot hear
anything. The behaviours that had code but no driver - cooking, clothing,
hunting, fishing, seeing one another, personality, skill, and both emotions -
now have one.

WHAT RUNS IN A LIVE SIMULATION
------------------------------
Verified as reached by Simulation::tick():

  Survival        hunger, thirst, energy, nutrition (energy, protein,
                  micronutrients), food spoilage, fatigue, body temperature,
                  exposure and shelter, injury and healing, death
  Calendar        a tick is two hours, a day twelve ticks, a season
                  twenty-four days and a year 1,152 ticks. A world opens in
                  spring; eight thousand ticks cover seven years and all four
                  seasons, where every run before this one ended on Year 0,
                  Day 4, having never left winter. The season decides regrowth,
                  daylight and the weather - winter snows a tenth of the time
                  and no other season snows at all. It does NOT decide the
                  temperature a tile reports: that is frozen at first touch, so
                  a clear day reads 18.667 C in every season alike. See
                  ISSUES_FOUND.md #4
  Lifecycle       aging, life stages, reproduction, pregnancy, birth,
                  nursing, inheritance of traits and behaviour, over a life of
                  eight or nine calendar years and thirty-odd seasons
  Behaviour       15 drives (the design document specifies 13; Thirst and
                  Protection came later), behaviour trees with weight learning,
                  goals,
                  plans, foraging, drinking, sheltering, exploration, cooking,
                  farming. Drives that look past this afternoon run five times
                  faster in an agent that is fed, watered, rested and warm and
                  a quarter as fast in one that is not
  Drives          nine of the fifteen read the conditions the design document
                  specifies - threat, darkness, weather, what is in the pack,
                  what the ground round about is bearing, whether a child has
                  strayed - and settle where the situation puts them rather
                  than climbing a clock. Six of the nine were pinned at their
                  ceiling for whole runs before this. Hunger, thirst and
                  tiredness still run on time
  Hierarchy       drives are ranked primary, secondary and tertiary, and
                  within the primary band the one that would kill soonest
                  wins, computed live from how long this agent could actually
                  last on what it has. Each drive is also gated behind the one
                  it follows - hunger before preparedness before luxury,
                  safety before shelter before protection, all four primaries
                  before reproduction - so nobody puts food by while hungry.
                  The ranked drives choose the action; the old fixed ladder is
                  kept only for drives that have no answer to offer
  Relationships   a bond runs from -1.0 to 1.0 and the name follows it: enemy,
                  rival, acquaintance, friend, and family which is never
                  renamed. Being near somebody and getting on with them are
                  dispositions that work over seasons and then stop - the
                  first at a familiar face, the second at a friend. What
                  takes two people past that is what they have done: a meal
                  shared, help offered, a grudge nursed, a blow struck. A
                  settlement contains hostile relationships now, which no
                  settlement in this project ever did before: measured over
                  eight worlds a side, relationships named rival or enemy went
                  from 0.00 per agent to 1.53 at 5.30 standard errors, and the
                  mean bond from 0.90 to 0.82. It costs fourteen more deaths
                  at 1.81 se, on a settlement with slightly more births and a
                  slightly higher peak - more turnover rather than failure,
                  and eight of eight still inhabited
  Trust           whose word an agent takes is decided from the bond, from
                  whether that one has been right before, and from what sort
                  of people the two of them are. An agent below the line
                  hears a claim and does not go and stand on it. A man who
                  would rather lie names a place a good walk from the real
                  one; it is found out by walking there and seeing bare
                  ground, and what it costs him depends on what he lied about
                  - a lie about food to a starving man is not the same as one
                  about a pile of rocks. Nothing consulted any of this before,
                  and no lie had ever been told in a running settlement.
                  Measured over eight worlds a side: 7.4% of pairs will not
                  take each other's word, nearly every agent has caught
                  somebody out, and the whole price is eight places in two
                  hundred and forty that an agent no longer knows of. Nothing
                  else moves within a standard error
  Emotion         fear and anger are appraisals: the strength of the thing in
                  front of the agent, scaled by how near it is, weighed
                  against what the agent can do about it - health, build,
                  armour, weapon, combat skill, nerve, and how past fights
                  went. What can be fought comes out as anger and what cannot
                  as fear, and both clear when the thing goes away. A need
                  that has gone unanswered frightens in proportion to how soon
                  it would kill, not to how loudly it asks
  Pressure        a denied drive presses harder the longer it waits, up to
                  fourfold, on both how fast it builds and how loudly it argues
                  - so a settlement that cannot feed somebody loses them to it.
                  Breeding waits on a surplus or a long settled stretch;
                  children have a quarter to a half of an adult's reserves
                  against a famine; ten days of unanswered hunger sends an
                  agent out of the country it is in
  Learning        every action's outcome is recorded against the kind of
                  undertaking it was and shifts what the agent will try next.
                  Failures count for more than successes, nothing is dropped
                  before five attempts, and a hunter who never catches
                  anything stops hunting
  Land            every tile carries soil: nutrients, and two pools of dead
                  matter waiting to become more of it. What rots and how fast
                  depends on how dense it was alive and how wet the ground is
  Trades          a hand gets better at what it does and worse at what it does
                  not. Finding a thing pays a pittance once; doing the work
                  pays properly; a level costs more the higher it goes; a trade
                  unpractised for a year begins to go, though never below
                  apprentice. What a hand is worth runs from half to double,
                  and decides what comes off a field per trip and whether a
                  garment is finished or spoiled in the making. Most people end
                  up mediocre at several things and a few genuinely good at one
  Personality     a founder is drawn with three to five compatible traits out
                  of sixty-odd, and everybody born afterwards takes after their
                  two parents with a chance of mutation. Blindness, deafness
                  and muteness arise by birth rather than by founding. Forty
                  founders are forty different people. What the traits reach so
                  far is how somebody feels about what happened, who they get
                  on with, what they pass on in gossip, and how long a plan
                  they will countenance - not yet what they choose to do
  Fishing         fish come up the river on the season rather than growing back
                  out of what is left of them, so a reach fished out fills
                  again from the catchment. Agents go to the water when they
                  want food, learn for themselves whether it pays, and what is
                  left of a fish is worth forty times a unit of crop to a
                  field - it was grown at sea, not on the settlement's ground
  Nutrient return four paths put matter back where growing took it out: what a
                  body passes after a meal, what spoils in a pack, what a body
                  is when it stops, and the roots, stalk and leaf a plant
                  leaves in the tile it grew in. Rot loses two fifths of what
                  it works on, so the loop turns and loses on every turn
  Flora           about two hundred plants stand in a new world, each growing
                  on whichever of water, light and nutrient it has least of;
                  standing foliage shades what is under it and sheds leaf fall
                  that in time becomes soil
  Farming         agents break open grass into fields and sow them. A field
                  gets at two and a half times as much of what the soil holds
                  and carries a heavier crop; it does not grow anything faster
                  than that plant's kind can grow
  Practices       agents work muck-spreading out for themselves - tried out of
                  curiosity, judged by whether the ground visibly improves,
                  dropped if it never does, and picked up by whoever watches
  Family          parents keep their children close, going to one that has
                  strayed and running to one that something is stalking;
                  children pick up skill experience watching the adults around
                  them, and three times as much watching their own parents
  Fire            agents gather wood, build and light campfires, and cook at
                  them; only meat, fish and grain are improved by a fire, and
                  anything else put over one is ruined - worthless, unsafe to
                  eat and smelling of it. How often a cook burns a batch falls
                  with practice, from one in five to none
  Clothing        agents gather flax, cotton and bark, make garments and wear
                  them. What a garment is worth follows its material - fur and
                  wool best, plant fibre next, bark last - and the hand that
                  made it. Wood is kept back for fires before it goes into
                  clothes
  Hunting         agents go after animals for the skins, and eat what comes
                  with them. A hunter has to be within a spear's throw, an
                  unarmed one leaves the dangerous animals alone, and what is
                  killed is butchered into meat, hides, leather and wool
  Ecology         herds are held down by the predators that live off them, and
                  by the ground they graze; predators that cannot find prey
                  starve, widen what they will take, and turn on the people
                  living beside them; a species wiped out here or
                  hunted down to a quarter of its peak is slowly replaced by
                  animals wandering in from off the map
  Perception      agents see one another, which observational learning is
                  gated on and had never had; sight of terrain, resources and
                  buildings within 25 tiles,
                  refreshed every tick and negated by the Blind trait; smell
                  scaled to what a thing actually gives off - a berry about
                  2 tiles, water 3, flesh 6, rot 9-20, cooking the full 25;
                  sharing of known resource locations between agents. Food
                  over a lit fire is the loudest smell there is, and now
                  something an agent can actually meet
  Social          proximity relationships, social interactions, gossip,
                  observational learning, shared knowledge, technology
                  discovery and spread
  World           terrain, climate, weather, seasons, resources and their
                  regeneration, buildings, crafting, fauna, flora, combat,
                  obstacle-aware pathfinding
  Persistence     save, load, autosave with checkpoint rotation
  Display         ASCII renderer, egui GUI (src/bin/gui.rs)


BUILT BUT NOT CONNECTED
-----------------------
These modules are implemented and tested in isolation, but nothing in the
simulation loop drives them. They are libraries awaiting a caller, not
missing features:

  analytics::web_api        ApiServer has zero call sites anywhere
  analytics::events         EventBus used only by its own tests
  analytics::replay         SessionRecorder used only by its own tests
  analytics::storage        StorageManager used only by its own tests
  analytics::metrics        \  work when a caller drives them; two examples
  analytics::emergence       > (ascii_simulation, phase4_analytics) show how,
  analytics::performance    /  but Simulation::tick does not feed them

  Zoning and territory      Building placement reads zone and territory
                            bonuses, but nothing outside tests ever creates a
                            zone or claims territory, so both are always empty

  Hearing                   Nothing feeds sounds from the world, so every
                            sound-derived percept is a dead path.


KNOWN GAPS IN BEHAVIOUR
-----------------------
  - Clothing costs about as much as it returns in this climate. Over forty
    worlds it halves how often agents are cold (28% to 16%) and warms them
    half a degree, at three points of the fed population and three percent of
    the population itself. The material is scarce and the weather is mild.
  - Hunting is worth it for a minority. Over forty worlds it puts 44 agents
    of 862 into fur, hide or leather - which nothing else can - at two points
    of the fed population and about eight percent of the population itself.
    There are only a handful of animals in a world, so most agents never get
    a skin.
  - Fauna is stable in most worlds but not all. Over forty worlds, predators
    are still alive at the end in thirty-six and herds stay bounded in
    thirty-three; the rest lose their predators and run to the population cap.
  - A settlement lasts, and now settles rather than slides. Four of four were
    still there at thirty thousand ticks, running to a peak of 226 and holding
    154 of it, on ground worked from about 0.55 fertility down to 0.27. Before
    the ground had a way back they peaked at 212 and ended at 53 on 0.058. The
    soil still runs down - rot loses two fifths of everything it works on, so
    fertility is falling in three of the four worlds at the end - but slowly
    enough that the settlement outlives the run.
  - Agents now feel about what is in front of them. Fear and anger are
    appraisals - the strength of the thing weighed against what this agent
    can do about it - rather than readings off the survival drives, and what
    happened in past fights scales the estimate. Over three worlds and 41,556
    agent-samples, mean fear ran 0.044 and mean anger 0.298, with 1.94% of
    samples wanting to flee and 22.8% wanting to fight, against a previous
    state of 0.01-0.06 fear, zero anger and nobody ever doing either. At eight
    worlds a side the appraisal alone did not change what a settlement is
    worth, because nothing read the feeling.
  - And the feelings now reach the hands. A frightened agent puts ground
    between itself and the thing; an angry one strikes at what is within
    arm's reach. Grudges against people work the same way - whether an agent
    squares up to somebody it cannot stand or keeps clear of them is the same
    appraisal - and nearly all the anger in this model turned out to be a
    grudge: of 22,802 samples ready to fight, anger at people ran to 0.806
    and anger at creatures to 0.025. Agents come to blows about ten times as
    often as before, and of 238 survivors 23 now reckon themselves worse in a
    fight for what happened, where that branch had nobody in it at all.
    It costs a settlement about fifteen percent of its end population, on a
    reading that does not clear one standard error at eight worlds a side but
    moves the same way on every population measure
  - Populations grow but have not been studied over very long runs. Nobody
    has characterised what happens past a few tens of thousands of ticks.


SUGGESTED NEXT STEPS
--------------------
  1. Re-measure whether personality now tells. The two things that blocked it
     are both gone: drives are ranked and choose the action first rather than
     being consulted at the thirteenth of thirteen fixed priorities, and the
     three permanently pegged drives have come off their ceilings. Foraging
     fell from 79 percent of everything a settlement does to 25, and
     Action::Build and Action::Socialize are chosen for the first time. The
     fourteen-world reading that found nothing above 1.4 standard errors was
     taken against the old ladder and no longer describes the model. See
     ISSUES_FOUND.md #11.
  2. Give agents a reason to need each other. Everyone still does everything,
     so no agent is ever the one who has what another wants. The fishery is
     the first thing in the model that is place-bound - you have to be at the
     water - which is the raw material for a division of labour, and a
     division of labour is what would make the social graph carry weight now
     that it has hostile edges in it.
  3. Seed world generation so the six flaky tests become deterministic and
     runs become reproducible.
  4. Decide the fate of the unconnected analytics: wire metrics, emergence
     detection and performance monitoring into Simulation::tick behind a
     config flag, or move them out of the crate.
  5. Give agents a way to make and keep tools, stores and anything decorative.
     Now that the drives read their specified conditions, the three that ask
     for those things sit high because nothing in the world answers them:
     thirty agents at eight thousand ticks held no tools and nothing fine
     between them. See ISSUES_FOUND.md #5.
  6. Let a settlement break new ground. The nutrient return has taken the
     overshoot-and-slide off the table, but two of the three things that
     sharpened it are untouched. A spent field still counts as a field:
     fields_within counts cultivated tiles rather than producing ones, and a
     tile carrying a resource node cannot be tilled again, so exhausted fields
     inside the walking radius stop a settlement opening new ones for good
     while the map around it is still fertile. And nobody has ever died of
     hunger - is_starving needs 1,440 ticks without food and death 10,080,
     which is most of a life. See ISSUES_FOUND.md #3.
  7. Decide whether plugins/minecraft_survival should exist at all: it now
     compiles and its tests pass, but it duplicates
     src/environment/minecraft_survival.rs. Either merge what is unique to it
     (Perlin world generation, water structures) into the in-tree module, or
     keep it as the worked example of writing a plugin.
  8. Clean up the build warnings and the committed
     src/analytics/mod.rs.backup.
  9. Characterise long-run behaviour: population dynamics, technology
     spread, and settlement patterns over 100k+ ticks.
